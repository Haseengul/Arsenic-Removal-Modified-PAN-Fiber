# Synthesis and Characterization of Chemically Modified Polyacrylonitrile (PAN) Fiber for Arsenic Removal from Synthetic Water

## Project Overview

This final-year Chemical Engineering project focused on the synthesis, chemical modification, and characterization of Polyacrylonitrile (PAN) fiber for arsenic removal from synthetic water.

The study investigated chemically modified PAN fiber as an adsorbent for As(V) removal using a continuous fixed-bed column system.

The project combined adsorbent preparation, material characterization, adsorption experiments, and arsenic concentration analysis to evaluate the potential of chemically modified PAN fiber for water treatment applications.

---

## Problem Statement

Arsenic contamination in drinking water is a serious environmental and public health concern, particularly in groundwater-affected regions.

The presence of arsenic in water can create significant health risks, while conventional treatment methods may face challenges in effectively removing arsenic at low concentrations.

Therefore, there is a need for affordable, efficient, and sustainable adsorbent materials for arsenic removal.

This project investigated chemically modified PAN fiber as a potential adsorbent for removing arsenic from synthetic water.

---

## Aim

To remove arsenic from synthetic water using chemically modified Polyacrylonitrile (PAN) fiber as an adsorbent.

---

## Objectives

- To develop chemically modified Polyacrylonitrile (PAN) fiber.
- To characterize the chemically modified PAN fiber.
- To analyze and evaluate the efficiency of chemically modified PAN fiber for As(V) removal through column studies.

---

## Scope of Work

The project focused on:

- Chemical modification of Polyacrylonitrile (PAN) fiber.
- Characterization of the modified fiber using FTIR and SEM.
- Iron loading of the modified PAN fiber.
- Application of the modified PAN fiber in a fixed-bed column.
- Investigation of arsenic removal from synthetic water.
- Evaluation of different adsorption operating parameters.
- Analysis of arsenic concentration using Atomic Absorption Spectroscopy (AAS).

---

## Methodology

The overall methodology consisted of the following stages:

### 1. Adsorbent Preparation

PAN fiber was chemically modified using Dimethylacetamide (DMAC).

The modification process involved controlled heating and stirring conditions.

The modified PAN fiber was subsequently subjected to iron loading to prepare the adsorbent used in the column experiments.

### 2. Iron Loading

The modified PAN fiber was loaded with ferric chloride hexahydrate (FeCl₃·6H₂O).

The iron-loading process involved preparation of the iron solution, impregnation of the modified PAN fiber, controlled stirring, pH adjustment, aging, washing, and drying.

### 3. Characterization of Adsorbent

The prepared adsorbent was characterized using:

- Fourier Transform Infrared Spectroscopy (FTIR)
- Scanning Electron Microscopy (SEM)

### 4. Fixed-Bed Column Study

A continuous fixed-bed adsorption column was used to investigate arsenic removal.

The major operating parameters investigated were:

- Influent flow rate
- Adsorbent bed height
- Initial solution pH

### 5. Arsenic Analysis

Atomic Absorption Spectroscopy (AAS) was used to analyze arsenic concentration in the experimental samples.

---

## Characterization Techniques

### FTIR — Fourier Transform Infrared Spectroscopy

Fourier Transform Infrared Spectroscopy (FTIR) was used to identify chemical bonds and functional groups in the material and investigate changes associated with chemical modification.

### SEM — Scanning Electron Microscopy

Scanning Electron Microscopy (SEM) was used to examine the surface morphology of the PAN fiber and observe changes in surface structure following modification and adsorption.

### AAS — Atomic Absorption Spectroscopy

Atomic Absorption Spectroscopy (AAS) was used for arsenic concentration analysis during the adsorption experiments.

---

## Fixed-Bed Column Study

Continuous fixed-bed column experiments were performed to evaluate arsenic removal using chemically modified PAN fiber.

The study investigated the effect of different adsorption parameters, including:

| Parameter | Investigated Purpose |
|---|---|
| Flow rate | Study the effect of influent flow conditions |
| Bed height | Study the effect of adsorbent bed depth |
| Initial pH | Study the influence of solution pH on arsenic removal |

The experiments were used to investigate arsenic adsorption and breakthrough behavior under different operating conditions.
